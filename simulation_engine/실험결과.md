  - Same system prompt (role, personality priors, incentives)

  - No sycophancy detection or envelope monitoring

**OCEAN Findings**:

1. **E-trait (best)**: The engine's persona controller is most effective at maintaining extraversion patterns. Behavioral features like `turn_initiation_ratio` and `question_ratio` are well-calibrated by the controller.

2. **O-trait**: Strong improvement. The engine generates more ideas (0.370 vs 0.101, +267%) and hypotheticals (0.029 vs 0.015, +88%), driving openness fidelity.

3. **C-trait (hardest)**: Both conditions struggle with conscientiousness (0.22+ error). This is the most abstract OCEAN trait to operationalize via dialogue features. The dynamic calibration (relative-to-baseline) helps but C remains structurally difficult.

4. **N-trait (RLHF constraint)**: The only trait where naive marginally outperforms. LLMs resist generating anxious/neurotic behavior due to RLHF safety training. The engine's persona controller actively steers toward neurotic behavior via `self_doubt_count`, `reassurance_seeking_count`, and `emotional_word_count`, but RLHF suppression creates a floor effect where both conditions converge to near-zero on these features.

### 4.4 Scaling Observations

1. **Optimal actor count is 5**: Lowest drift (0.1646), lowest envelope violations (2.002), best balance of convergence (0.650) and diversity (0.561).

2. **10-actor stability**: SD drops to 0.0096 (half of 3-actor's 0.0201). More actors = more consistent results via averaging.

3. **Convergence-diversity tradeoff at scale**: At 10 actors, convergence jumps to 0.816 while diversity drops to 0.339. This is the structural "many voices converge" effect — more actors leads to opinion averaging.

4. **Envelope violations scale with actors**: 2.009 → 2.002 → 2.155. The 10-actor increase (d=+0.432 from 3→10, medium effect) suggests per-actor normalization is needed for large groups.

5. **Engine advantage diminishes at scale**: Delta shrinks from -0.0117 (3-actor) to -0.0083 (10-actor). This is expected — with more actors, each individual's trait deviation is averaged out, leaving less room for the controller to improve.

## 10. Weaknesses and Failure Modes

### 10.1 Engine Weaknesses vs Naive

| Metric | Engine | Naive | Delta | Concern |
|--------|--------|-------|-------|---------|
| Relationship Overshoot | 0.229 | 0.185 | +23.9% | Engine over-tracks relationship dynamics |
| Repetition Rate | 0.0024 | 0.000 | +inf | Marginal but nonzero repetition |
| N-trait Error | 0.170 | 0.168 | +1.1% | Engine slightly worse on neuroticism |

### 10.2 Root Cause Analysis

**Relationship Overshoot (+23.9%)**: The engine's relationship tracking system detects and amplifies relationship changes. When actors have mild positive/negative interactions, the engine's relationship state updates can overshoot, creating unrealistically strong relationship shifts. The `relationship_shift_rate` is nearly identical (engine 0.282 vs naive 0.280), suggesting the issue is not shift frequency but shift magnitude.

**N-trait Regression (+1.1%)**: The engine's persona controller tries to steer toward neurotic behaviors (self-doubt, reassurance-seeking, emotional volatility) but hits the RLHF floor. The added steering creates a subtle conflict between the controller's target and the LLM's safety training, producing marginally worse results than the naive "don't try" approach.

**CLOSING Phase Drift (0.179)**: Both conditions show highest drift in CLOSING. All actors converge to "summarize and agree" patterns regardless of their persona. This is a structural LLM tendency that even the engine's phase cues don't fully overcome.

## 12. Conclusions

### 12.1 Main Findings

1. **The simulation engine significantly improves persona fidelity over naive LLM generation.** Across 4,080 runs on 20 diverse scenarios, the engine reduces persona drift by 5.7% (Cohen's d = -0.94 paired) and envelope violations by 9.9%. Both results are statistically significant (p < 10^-48).

2. **The improvement is universal**: Engine wins on drift in 100% of scenarios and 95% of scenarios on envelope violations. No scenario shows naive superiority on the primary metric.

3. **The engine qualitatively changes dialogue**: 2.7x more ideas, 69% fewer sycophantic acknowledgments, 37% more disagreements. Engine-generated conversations are substantively richer and less formulaic.

4. **Scaling works**: The engine maintains its advantage from 3 to 10 actors. 5-actor configurations are optimal (lowest drift, lowest envelope). 10-actor runs show increased convergence (structural limitation) but halved variance.

5. **RLHF remains the fundamental bottleneck**: Neuroticism and certain agreeableness behaviors (self-doubt, reassurance-seeking) remain near-zero regardless of persona instructions. This is a generative model limitation, not an engine limitation.

### 12.2 Limitations

1. **Single generator model**: All runs use DeepSeek V3 (0324). Results may differ with other base models.
2. **Evaluator dependency**: Trait drift is measured by the engine's own evaluation pipeline (30-feature extraction + OCEAN scoring). External human evaluation would strengthen claims.
3. **Relationship overshoot**: The engine's relationship tracking produces 24% more overshoot than naive — a known area for calibration improvement.
4. **C-trait ceiling**: Conscientiousness error (0.22+) remains high for both conditions. This trait may need fundamentally different behavioral features.

### 12.3 Recommended Code/Design Fixes

1. **Relationship overshoot calibration**: Reduce relationship state update magnitude in `controller.py` `_update_relationship_state()`. Current delta multiplier is too aggressive for subtle interactions.

2. **Action validity rate (0.641)**: Tighten `_validate_action()` in `controller.py` to catch more structurally invalid actions before they reach the dialogue generation step.

3. **Feedback utilization (0.217)**: Strengthen the action feedback injection in `actor.py` `_build_system_prompt()` — currently feedback is appended but may be buried by longer context windows.

4. **10-actor envelope normalization**: Add per-actor envelope violation normalization in `metrics.py` for `n > 5` actor scenarios to prevent violation count from scaling with group size.

5. **CLOSING phase anti-convergence**: Add explicit CLOSING-phase prompt injection to prevent "summarize and agree" patterns, similar to the TENSION phase's disagreement cues.
